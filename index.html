Urban Heat Risk in Brussels

A remote-sensing and GIS prototype for identifying urban heat-risk hotspots and supporting cooling actions.

ProblemDataMethod ResultsFeedback
01

Problem

Brussels experiences an urban heat-island effect: built-up areas can become warmer than less urbanised surroundings. Hard surfaces such as concrete and asphalt absorb heat, while limited vegetation and reduced air circulation can intensify heat.

The City of Brussels reports that temperatures can be up to 10°C higher in certain neighbourhoods and describes cooling-island approaches based on vegetation, water, shade, suitable materials and better air circulation.

Target user: municipality, urban planner or city decision-maker.

03

Method

GIS and remote-sensing workflow

  1. Acquire cloud-screened Landsat and Sentinel-2 images for Brussels.
  2. Pre-process imagery and clip it to the study area.
  3. Use Landsat surface-temperature data to obtain LST.
  4. Calculate NDVI from Sentinel-2 to represent vegetation.
  5. Calculate a built-up indicator such as NDBI where appropriate.
  6. Standardise the indicators and combine them into an urban heat-risk index.
  7. Overlay the result with parks, roads, buildings and cooling resources.
  8. Publish the final decision-support result on the web page.

Example decision logic

  • High temperature + low vegetation: investigate trees, shade and green infrastructure.
  • High built-up density: investigate cool materials, green roofs and shaded public areas.
  • Existing green/cooling area: preserve and connect it to other cooling corridors.
  • Reported hot spot: compare the observation with the GIS layer before planning an intervention.
04

Results

Decision-support idea

A municipality could identify priority areas, inspect the factors behind the risk, compare existing cooling resources and collect observations from residents or field teams.
